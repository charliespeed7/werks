# WERKS Master Context

## Working definition

WERKS is an AI business partner for small service businesses and micro-businesses. It lives primarily in the owner's phone and helps answer customers, capture leads, manage bookings, prepare quotes, issue invoices, chase payment, follow up customers, organise the working day, and preserve business memory.

WERKS is not positioned as a CRM, a generic chatbot, or AI software for its own sake. It is an operating layer for businesses whose owners spend too much time on administration instead of paid work.

## Initial market

The initial wedge is UK trades, with plumbers and heating engineers as the leading candidate.

Adjacent markets include electricians, builders, decorators, roofers, gardeners, landscapers, cleaners, locksmiths, mobile mechanics, florists, dog groomers, personal trainers, salons, and tattoo studios.

## Core customer reality

The owner is often also the operator. The business commonly runs through a phone, van, diary, WhatsApp, SMS, email, Google Calendar, Google Maps, a banking app, and perhaps accounting software.

The primary competitor is not necessarily another software platform. It is memory, fragmented communication, paper, spreadsheets, and unpaid evening or weekend administration.

## Core promise

Never miss a customer. Never forget a follow-up. Never lose Sunday to admin.

WERKS should save meaningful time while helping the owner win, retain, and collect revenue.

## Product thesis

The model is not the moat. The moat is the accumulated workflow library, business memory, trust, integrations, decision history, and the habit of running the business through WERKS.

WERKS should become proactive rather than merely responsive. It should identify what needs attention, suggest the next best action, and act automatically within clearly defined permissions.

## Initial capabilities

1. Dedicated business phone number
2. AI call answering and lead capture
3. SMS and WhatsApp support
4. Calendar and booking management
5. Quote drafting and acceptance follow-up
6. Invoice drafting and payment chasing
7. Daily operational summary
8. Customer and job memory
9. Approval controls and audit trail

## Long-term capability layers

1. Assistant: helps with discrete admin tasks
2. Operator: completes repeatable operational work
3. Manager: coordinates the day and recommends decisions
4. Business Partner: improves revenue, cash flow, and capacity
5. Autonomous Business Layer: runs most non-skilled work within agreed authority

## Long-term expansion

- Procurement and supplier ordering
- Parts identification and image recognition
- Route and capacity optimisation
- Reviews, referrals, local search, and customer reactivation
- Lending, insurance, payments, tax, bookkeeping, and payroll
- Team, vehicle, compliance, and inventory management
- Cross-business fulfilment and trusted subcontractor networks

## Product principles

- Phone-first
- Voice-first where valuable
- WhatsApp-native
- Low setup effort
- Useful within the first hour
- Calm and simple
- Proactive, not noisy
- Transparent about what happened
- Safe by default
- Human approval where risk is material
- Every action auditable
- Revenue and time saved are more important than feature usage

## MVP boundary

The MVP should test whether a small business owner will trust WERKS to interact with real customers and take low-risk operational actions.

The MVP should not attempt autonomous procurement, lending, complex accounting, multi-team optimisation, or field diagnosis.

## MVP success measures

- Leads captured that would otherwise have been missed
- Jobs booked
- Follow-ups completed
- Time saved per week
- Quote response time
- Invoice collection time
- Owner approval rate
- Incorrect-action rate
- Customer satisfaction
- Weekly retained usage

## Decision rule for every feature

Ask:

1. Does this reduce owner admin?
2. Does this prevent lost revenue?
3. Does this improve customer responsiveness?
4. Can it be made trustworthy and auditable?
5. Does it strengthen WERKS's business memory or workflow advantage?

If not, it should probably wait.

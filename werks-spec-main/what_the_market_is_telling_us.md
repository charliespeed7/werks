## Purpose

This file notices what changed in the business this week — not just what happened. The highest signal customer insight is a change in behaviour, not a summary of activity.

It should be updated daily by an agent that reads from the places where customer truth lives, identifies a pattern, provides the receipts behind it, and states the product or GTM decision it might affect.

## Daily Format

Each morning, the agent should write one entry in the following format:

---

### [Date]

**The pattern I noticed today:**

[One or two sentences describing a change or pattern in the data]

**The receipts:**

- [Specific data point 1 — e.g. "3 of 5 text-backs yesterday resulted in a job booking"]
- [Specific data point 2 — e.g. "Average invoice-to-cash time dropped from 41 to 28 days this week"]
- [Specific data point 3 — e.g. "Engineer edited 2 of 8 quotes before approving — down from 6 of 8 last week"]

**What this might mean:**

[One sentence on the product or GTM decision this could affect]

**What I'd do about it:**

[One specific recommendation — e.g. "Look at the 2 quotes that were edited — what did the engineer change? If it's pricing, the quote logic may need adjustment."]

## What This File Is NOT

- It is not a dashboard. Dashboards show what happened. This file shows what changed.
- It is not a weekly report. It is a daily signal — the sooner a pattern is noticed, the sooner it can be acted on.
- It is not a summary. Summaries are noise. This file should contain the single highest-signal insight from the day, with evidence.
- It is not a replacement for talking to customers. The richest data source will always be a conversation with the engineer.

## The Questions This File Should Answer Over Time

1. **Are engineers approving drafts without editing them?** (D6a retention signal)
2. **Is invoice-to-cash time improving?** (D2 core metric)
3. **Does the text-back win emergency jobs?** (D1 — segmented by hour and urgency)
4. **What do engineers edit when they do edit?** (Product signal — what's wrong with the drafts)
5. **What do customers say in the text-back that the agent didn't handle well?** (Escalation and red line signal for D6b)
6. **Is the annual service loop generating reminders that convert?** (D5 — only answerable after 12+ months)
7. **What language are new enquiries using?** (Does the market's vocabulary shift over time?)

## Pre-Launch Section (until first customer goes live)

Until WERKS has a live customer, this section captures what the market is telling us through research, not product data.

### [2026-08-04] Competitor desk pass complete

**The pattern I noticed today:**

The UK price anchor for AI answering services is higher than the spec assumed — £45 headline but £65–100 real cost with channels and overage. No competitor runs the full job lifecycle through AI. The incumbent window for steps 1–3 is already closed (Jobber, HCP shipped AI answering), but steps 4–8 are open.

**The receipts:**

- Down To Earth AI: £45/mo headline, but £65–100/mo with WhatsApp/SMS and overage
- 0 competitors offer AI-driven quoting, invoicing, or payment chasing
- Jobber AI Receptionist shipped August 2025 — live for nearly a year
- Housecall Pro CSR AI is live and answering calls
- 0 competitors have appliance-level memory

**What this might mean:**

Pricing can sit higher than the £45 anchor. The lifecycle gap is our position. Speed to quoting and collections matters more than speed to call answering — the incumbents already own that.

**What I'd do about it:**

Prioritise quoting and invoicing in the MVP roadmap. Don't compete on call answering — compete on cash collected and hours returned. Price above £65/mo, not above £45/mo.

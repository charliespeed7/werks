## Purpose

This document answers the question that probably decides whether the company works: **how do we acquire a one-to-three-van gas and heating business for less than its lifetime value?**

It is not in the original spec. It was added because the parallel tasks list flagged distribution as "the question that decides whether the company works" and noted three routes worth pricing before committing to any.

## Context

From `01_vision.md`:
- The wedge is UK gas and heating engineers, one to three vans
- Gas Safe Register lists roughly 70,000 registered businesses and 130,000 engineers
- The register is public and searchable by postcode — but it is a consumer lookup, not a downloadable file, and most registrants are sole traders whose contact data is personal data under UK GDPR
- WERKS is not sold as an AI receptionist — the pitch is cash collected and hours returned
- The target customer is the owner who runs the business on a phone, a diary and memory, and who has already declined or abandoned software

From `19_competitor_analysis.md`:
- The UK price anchor is £45 headline / £65–100 actual (with channels and overage)
- The lifecycle gap (steps 4–9) is unoccupied by any competitor
- The incumbent window for steps 1–3 is closed (Jobber, HCP have shipped AI answering)
- No competitor has appliance-level memory

The lifetime value question is simple arithmetic. If WERKS charges £120–200/mo and the average customer stays 18–24 months, LTV is £2,160–4,800. If we charge more or they stay longer (annual service loop driving retention), LTV rises. The acquisition cost needs to sit comfortably below that number — ideally below one month's revenue, so £120–200.

## Three Routes

### Route 1 — Trade Merchant Partnership

**The thesis:** Merchants know every gas engineer in a radius by name and by spend. They are the first place an engineer goes in the morning and the last place they think about pricing. A partnership — not an ad buy — puts WERKS in front of engineers at the counter, on the merchant app, and in the trade counter newsletter.

**Target merchants:**

| Merchant | Approx branches | Why |
|---|---|---|
| City Plumbing | 450+ UK branches | Largest UK plumbing/heating merchant. Direct reach to gas engineers. |
| Wolseley | 1,500+ UK branches | Largest trade merchant network. Strong relationships with heating engineers. |
| Plumbase | 130+ branches | Specialist plumbing/heating merchant. Smaller but highly targeted. |
| PTS Plumbing Trade Supplies | 300+ branches | BSS Group owned. Strong heating engineer base. |
| Jewsons / Builder Centre | 400+ branches | More generalist but significant footfall from trades. |
| Graham | 200+ branches | BSS Group owned. Heating-focused. |

**What a partnership looks like:**

1. **Counter presence.** A WERKS standee, leaflet, or QR code at the trade counter. Engineers spend 10–20 minutes waiting for materials. That is the dwell time.
2. **Merchant app integration.** City Plumbing and Wolseley both have trade apps with hundreds of thousands of users. A WERKS tile or notification inside the app reaches engineers without a separate download.
3. **Trade counter newsletter.** Merchants send regular communications to their trade account holders. A WERKS feature in that newsletter is a warm introduction, not a cold outreach.
4. **Account manager referral.** Merchant account managers visit engineers regularly. If WERKS gives them a one-minute pitch and a referral incentive, the account manager becomes a sales channel.
5. **Trade counter events.** Open days, product launches, manufacturer days. WERKS could attend or sponsor.

**Cost estimate:**

- Counter presence (standees, leaflets, QR codes across 50–100 branches): £2,000–5,000 for materials
- Newsletter feature: £500–2,000 per send (negotiable — may be free if positioned as a value-add for account holders)
- App integration: development cost on WERKS side, negotiation with merchant on commercial terms
- Account manager incentive: £50–100 per activated engineer (referral fee)
- Trade counter events: £500–1,500 per event

**Estimated cost per acquisition (CPA):** £50–200 per engineer, depending on conversion rate. If 1% of exposed engineers convert and each branch's counter sees 200 engineers/week, 100 branches = 20,000 weekly impressions. At 1% conversion over 12 weeks: ~2,400 trials. If 20% become paying customers: ~480 customers at a cost of roughly £50–100 each.

**Advantages:**
- Reaches the exact ICP at the exact moment they are thinking about work
- Merchant endorsement carries trust — a referral from Wolseley means more than a Facebook ad
- Low cost per impression; high dwell time at counter
- Merchants have an incentive to help their trade customers be more efficient — an engineer who gets paid faster buys more materials

**Disadvantages:**
- Merchant partnerships are slow to negotiate (3–6 months minimum)
- Merchants may want exclusivity, which limits other channels
- Branch managers have autonomy — national deal doesn't guarantee local execution
- Hard to measure attribution (which branch, which leaflet, which event)

**Lead time:** 3–6 months to negotiate and launch a national partnership. Can pilot with a single branch or region in 4–8 weeks.

**Recommendation:** Start conversations with City Plumbing and Wolseley now. The lead time is the issue — if this is the route, starting later adds months at the worst moment. A single-branch pilot in the south east could test the conversion rate cheaply before committing nationally.

### Route 2 — Trade Creators on TikTok and Instagram

**The thesis:** Gas engineers follow other gas engineers on social media. Peer credibility — a fellow engineer saying "this saved my Sunday" — is worth more than any advertisement. Test with three paid posts before building a content operation.

**Target creators (from the influencer research):**

| Creator | Handle | Platform | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ian Briggs | @irbheating | IG | Harlow, Essex. Full-time plumber. Exactly our ICP. Recognisable. |
| Luke Stanyer | @bluebulldoguk | IG | Walsall. Heating Installer of the Year finalist. Small business energy. |
| Dan Goulding | @centralheatking | TikTok/YouTube | 54K TikTok, 12.5K YouTube. Weekly heating engineer content. Brand deals. |
| Mark Tiff | @mjtiff_plumbingandheating | IG/Podcast | Trade Legends podcast host. Reach to the whole trade, not just plumbing. |
| Sam Ferry | @professorplumb_ | TikTok | 78.9K TikTok. Plumber-turned-influencer. Lower cost, high engagement. |
| Pete Gilbert | @beaplumber | IG | Viral plumbing sensation. Hundreds of thousands of followers. Bigger reach, higher cost. |

**What it looks like:**

1. **Phase 1 — Test with three paid posts (4–6 weeks).** Pay three Tier 2 creators (Ian, Luke, Dan) to make one post each showing their real Sunday admin pain and how WERKS handles it. Budget: £200–500 per post (Tier 2 rates). Total test: £600–1,500.
2. **Phase 2 — Measure.** Track signups from each post using referral codes or UTM links. If CPA is under £150 and the signups are the right ICP (gas engineers, 1–3 vans, not already on Jobber/HCP), scale.
3. **Phase 3 — Scale to 10–15 creators.** Add Tier 1 creators (Pete Gilbert, Matthew Marciniak) at higher rates (£1,000–3,000 per post). Build a content operation — WERKS's own channel featuring real engineer stories.
4. **Phase 4 — Always-on content.** Regular posts, behind-the-scenes, before/after stories. Make WERKS the product the trade talks about.

**Cost estimate:**

| Phase | Activity | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 test posts (Tier 2 creators) | £600–1,500 |
| 2 | Measurement | £0 (UTM links, referral codes) |
| 3 | 10–15 creators (mixed tiers) | £3,000–15,000 |
| 4 | Ongoing content operation | £500–2,000/month |

**Estimated CPA:** £50–200 per activated engineer in Phase 1. If a Tier 2 creator has 20K engaged followers and 2% click through, 400 visitors. If 10% sign up for a trial and 20% convert to paying: 8 customers from one post at £500 cost = £62.50 CPA.

**Advantages:**
- Fast to start — can test in weeks, not months
- Peer credibility — an engineer trusts another engineer more than a brand
- Targetable — can choose creators whose audience is exactly the ICP
- Scalable — if it works, add more creators; if it doesn't, stop
- Compounding — creator content stays online and continues to drive signups

**Disadvantages:**
- Creator audiences are mixed — not all followers are gas engineers
- Attribution is imperfect — signups may not match to a specific post
- Quality of content varies — needs a brief, not just "make a post"
- Creator rates are rising as more brands enter the trade space

**Lead time:** 2–4 weeks to Phase 1. Can start immediately.

**Recommendation:** Start Phase 1 now. Three posts at £500 each is the cheapest, fastest test of any route on this list. If it doesn't work, the learning cost is £1,500. If it works, scale aggressively before competitors discover the same creators.

### Route 3 — Manufacturer Installer Schemes

**The thesis:** Worcester Bosch, Vaillant, Baxi and Ideal all run accredited installer schemes. These schemes have public directories of registered installers, enforce minimum standards, and are exactly the kind of business WERKS is targeting — accredited, professional, 1–3 vans, doing boilers and annual services.

**Target schemes:**

| Scheme | Manufacturer | Approx registered installers | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worcester Bosch Accredited Installer | Worcester Bosch | 3,000–5,000 | Largest boiler manufacturer in the UK. Directory is public. Installers do high volumes of annual services. |
| Vaillant Advance | Vaillant | 2,000–4,000 | Premium manufacturer. Installers tend to be higher-end, more likely to value admin efficiency. |
| Baxi Works | Baxi | 2,000–3,000 | Mid-market. Strong installer network. |
| Ideal Registered Installers | Ideal Boilers | 1,500–2,500 | Smaller but still significant. |
| Glow-worm Club | Glow-worm | 1,000–2,000 | Smaller scheme. |

**What it looks like:**

1. **Directory scraping (lawful).** The directories are public. Installers list their business name, location, and sometimes phone and email. If the data is public and the contact is a business (not a personal sole trader), it may be usable for B2B outreach under UK GDPR PECR rules — but this needs legal sign-off before doing it. This is the same question raised in Item 12 of the parallel tasks list.
2. **Partnership with the manufacturer.** A deeper play: Worcester Bosch or Vaillant endorses WERKS as a recommended tool for their accredited installers. This is harder to negotiate but far more powerful — the manufacturer's endorsement is gold.
3. **Manufacturer installer events.** Annual installer days, product launches, training days. WERKS attends or sponsors.
4. **Co-marketing.** WERKS featured in the manufacturer's installer communications — email, app, portal.

**Cost estimate:**

| Activity | Cost |
|---|---|
| Directory research + legal sign-off | £500–1,500 (solicitor time) |
| Outreach campaign (email/phone) | £0–500 (if self-served) or £2,000–5,000 (if outsourced) |
| Manufacturer partnership negotiation | £0 (time cost, 3–6 months) |
| Installer event sponsorship | £1,000–5,000 per event |

**Estimated CPA:** £100–300 per engineer, depending on whether the manufacturer partnership is secured. Without a partnership (cold outreach from directory): £200–300. With a partnership (manufacturer-endorsed): £50–150.

**Advantages:**
- Highly targeted — accredited installers are exactly the ICP
- Public directories mean the addressable list is real and countable
- Manufacturer endorsement is the strongest possible trust signal in this market
- Installers in these schemes do annual services — the D5 retention loop is naturally present

**Disadvantages:**
- Legal complexity around using sole trader contact data (PECR/GDPR)
- Manufacturer partnerships are slow (6+ months) and require a product to show
- Directories may not include phone numbers or emails for all installers
- Cold outreach to sole traders has low response rates without a warm introduction

**Lead time:** 2–4 weeks for directory research + legal. 6+ months for manufacturer partnership.

**Recommendation:** Start the directory research now — it also serves Item 12 of the parallel tasks list (establishing what the lawful addressable list actually is). Defer manufacturer partnership conversations until WERKS has a product to show.

## Comparison

| | Route 1: Merchants | Route 2: Creators | Route 3: Manufacturers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed to start | 4–8 weeks (pilot) | 2–4 weeks | 2–4 weeks (research), 6+ months (partnership) |
| Estimated CPA | £50–200 | £50–200 | £50–300 |
| Trust signal | Merchant endorsement | Peer credibility | Manufacturer endorsement |
| Targeting precision | High (engineers at counter) | Medium (mixed audiences) | Very high (accredited installers) |
| Scalability | Medium (national deal needed) | High (add creators) | Medium (finite directories) |
| Attribution | Low | Medium (UTM/referral codes) | Medium |
| Lead time to full scale | 3–6 months | 1–2 months | 6–12 months |
| Risk | Merchant deal falls through | Creator content doesn't convert | Legal risk on outreach |

## Recommendation

**Run Route 2 first, Route 1 in parallel, Route 3 as research.**

1. **Route 2 (creators) — start now.** It is the cheapest, fastest test. £1,500 over 4 weeks tells us if peer-led acquisition works. If it does, scale. If it doesn't, we've lost almost nothing.

2. **Route 1 (merchants) — start conversations now, pilot in 8 weeks.** The lead time is the issue. If we wait until the product is ready, we add 3–6 months. A pilot with City Plumbing or Wolseley in one region can run while we build.

3. **Route 3 (manufacturers) — research now, partnerships later.** The directory research doubles as Item 12 (lawful addressable list). Manufacturer partnerships need a product to show, so they wait — but the research doesn't.

## Open Questions

- [ ] What are Tier 2 creator rates in the UK plumbing/heating niche specifically? The £200–500 estimate is from general trade influencer pricing, not negotiated quotes.

- [ ] Does the Gas Safe Register offer any bulk data or API access for registered businesses (as opposed to individual postcode lookups)? If so, it changes the addressable list question entirely.
- [ ] What is the realistic LTV assumption? If 18 months at £150/mo, LTV is £2,700. If the annual service loop extends retention to 36 months, LTV is £5,400. The CPA ceiling changes dramatically.

## What this cannot tell you

- **Which route will actually work.** All three CPAs are estimates. Only a live test answers this.
- **Whether the product itself converts.** Acquisition gets them to trial; the product gets them to stay. If the product doesn't deliver, the best acquisition channel in the world just burns money faster.
- **The second-order channel.** Word of mouth between engineers is likely the cheapest acquisition channel long-term, but it cannot be started — only earned.

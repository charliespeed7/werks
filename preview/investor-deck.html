05 — The thesis

The informal economy is the white space.

In the UK and US, trades businesses use spreadsheets, paper, or nothing. In Brazil and Africa, they run on messaging apps and cash. Nobody serves either.

Messaging and voice are the unlock.

01

No app to download

Werks isn't tied to one network — WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, SMS, or a phone call. It arrives in the thread the owner already has open, and it answers the phone they already carry.

02

Same product, repriced

Start in the UK: highest ARPU, clearest regulation. The same product, repriced and localised, reaches Brazil at £6 and Africa at £4 — where messaging is already the operating system for informal business.

Werks.
06 — Why now

None of this was buildable two years ago.

01

Voice is good enough

AI voice and text now hold a real customer call end to end. Two years ago they couldn't.

02

Messaging APIs are global

Business messaging APIs now reach every market we want, on the networks people already use.

03

The market is untouched

2 billion informal workers are the largest underserved software market on earth.

04

Agent commerce is coming

Werks positions the informal economy for it — with the context layer agents will have to run on.

For context: the global field-service software market runs $6.2B in 2026 to $23.6B by 2035 at 16% CAGR. Werks isn't competing for that market — it is reaching the businesses that have never bought software at all.
